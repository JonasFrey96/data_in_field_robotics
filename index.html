About the Workshop

Mobile robots, including legged robots, humanoids, and drones, are increasingly deployed in unstructured and demanding environments—from scaling rubble and inspecting industrial facilities to navigating disaster zones. These open-ended tasks require robustness to terrain, resilience under degraded sensing, and adaptability to diverse task complexity. On the path to maturity in mobile robotics, data and benchmarks have proven to be the core driving factor, as demonstrated in autonomous driving. There, large-scale datasets and task-specific benchmarks have propelled tasks such as localization, mapping and detection to reach remarkable levels of precision. However, for mobile robots in the field, we are still missing this ecosystem. Unlike passenger vehicles, collecting data in the field with these platforms poses significant challenges. The cost of collecting data is high, and these platforms cannot easily carry the full payload of sensors and ground-truth systems. This raises several key open questions: What kind of datasets will have lasting impact and be most useful for robotics? Which sensors are truly essential to capture the task complexity of these platforms? What level of calibration and ground-truth effort is required to advance research in these tasks? Most importantly: how do we design benchmarks that are apt for the unique tasks and failure modes of these robots beyond navigation on suburban streets? This workshop will bring together researchers from robotics, computer vision, and machine learning to define principles for the next generation of datasets and benchmarks. By rethinking dataset and benchmark design in light of the distinct requirements of embodied intelligence, we aim to accelerate progress not only in localization, but also in perception, mapping, and long-term autonomy in the wild.

13:40–14:00 — Lukas Rosenberger Schmid

Lukas Rosenberger Schmid is a tenure-track professor of machine intelligence at the University of Technology Nuremberg. He earned his PhD at ETH Zürich and subsequently worked as a postdoctoral fellow and research scientist in MIT’s SPARK Lab. His research focuses on robot perception and mapping in dynamic environments, including scene understanding, active perception and lifelong learning.

14:00–14:20 — Cherie Ho

Cherie Ho is a postdoctoral researcher at Stanford University, working with Professor Jeannette Bohg. Her research develops flexible and self-improving robots for messy, real-world environments. She studies how robots can build and use memory, predict and reason about consequences, and continually improve from their own experience, with applications in mobile manipulation and navigation. Cherie received her PhD in Robotics from Carnegie Mellon University, where she was advised by Professor Sebastian Scherer. She is a recipient of the Croucher Fellowship and has been recognized as a Robotics: Science and Systems (RSS) Pioneer and a Rising Star in Mechanical Engineering.

14:40–15:00 — James Forbes

James Forbes is a professor of mechanical engineering and William Dawson Scholar at McGill University, where he leads the DECAR laboratory. His research focuses on navigation and control, with applications spanning subsea inspection and mapping to multi-robot control. In addition, he serves as a Senior Editor of the International Journal of Robotics Research.

15:00–15:20 — Dr. Youming Qin

Dr. Youming Qin is the co-founder and CEO of Manifold Tech, a spatial-intelligence company developing hardware and platforms for large-scale 3D data capture and processing. The company’s technology is used for applications including robotics training, digital twins, industrial mapping and spatial AI. Dr. Qin co-founded Manifold Tech in 2022 and was named to the 2025 Forbes China 30 Under 30 list. Manifold Tech

16:30–16:50 — Stuart Young

Stuart Young is Senior Director at FieldAI Federal, specialising in autonomous systems for complex environments. He previously managed DARPA’s RACER program, advancing high-speed autonomous navigation in challenging off-road terrain. His research also addresses coordination among robot teams operating with limited sensing and unreliable communications.

16:50–17:10 — Tobias Fischer

Tobias Fischer is an Associate Professor and ARC Future Fellow at the Queensland University of Technology, and a Chief Investigator in its Centre for Robotics. His research focuses on robot localisation, visual place recognition and underwater perception. His doctoral thesis at Imperial College London received the UK Best PhD in Robotics Award.

We invite participants to push the boundaries of state estimation by submitting results on the GrandTour Benchmark. For full details on participation, submission guidelines, and evaluation criteria, please visit the COMFORT Challenge website.

The organizing committee reserves the right to adjust the prize ladder based on the submissions and the quality of results.

Call for Papers

We invite contributions related to data, benchmarking, state estimation, perception, and navigation for robots operating in challenging real-world environments. Relevant topics include, but are not limited to:

Topics of Interest

  • Data for field robotics
  • State estimation, LIO, VIO
  • Benchmarking and evaluation of field robotics
  • Multimodal sensing
  • Navigation and traversability
  • Autonomy stacks
  • Real-world deployment at scale

Submission Types and Review Process

We accept two types of submissions:

  1. Novel work: Submit an anonymous 2–4-page, double-column short paper, with unlimited references and appendices. Papers must follow the IROS 2026 formatting guidelines; we recommend this LaTeX template. Submissions will undergo double-blind peer review. Authors of accepted papers will then prepare a poster for an interactive presentation at the workshop.
  2. Previously published work: Work already published or accepted at IROS or another venue should be submitted as a one-page poster. These submissions will be reviewed only for their relevance to the workshop.

Poster format: Posters should be A0 size; both portrait and landscape are accepted.

Workshop papers are non-archival: they are not part of the official IROS proceedings, so submitting here does not prevent you from publishing the work elsewhere. Submit through the DFR Workshop venue on OpenReview. Additional venue information is available on the OpenReview forum.
